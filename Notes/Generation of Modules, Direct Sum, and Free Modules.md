**Def:** Let $M$ be an $R$-module and let $N_1, \dots, N_n$ be submodules of $M$.
- The sum of $N_1, \dots, N_n$ is the set of all finite sums of elements from the sets $N_i$: $\{a_1 + \dots + a_n\mid a_i \in N_i, i \in \{1, \dots, n\}\}$. Denote this sum by $N_1 + \dots + N_n$ or $\sum_{i = 1}^n N_i$. 
- For any subset $A$ of $M$ let $$RA := \{r_1 a_1 + \dots + r_n a_n \mid r_1, \dots, r_n \in a_1, \dots, a_n \in A, n \in \Bbb N^+\}$$(where by convention $RA = \{0\}$ when $A = \varnothing$). If $A$ is a finite set $\{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$ we shall write $\sum_{i = 1}^n Ra_i = Ra_1 + Ra_2 + \dots + Ra_n$ for $RA$. Call $RA$ the *submodule of $M$ generated by $A$*. If $N$ is a submodule of $M$ (possibly $N = M$) and $N = RA$, for some subset $A$ of $M$, we call $A$ a *set of generators* or *generating set* for $N$, and we say $N$ is *generated by $A$.*
- A *submodule* $N$ of $M$ (possibly $N = M$) is *finitely generated* if there is some finite subset $A$ of $M$ such that $N = RA$, that is, if $N$ is generated by some finite set.
- A submodule $N$ of $M$ (possibly $N = M$) is *cyclic* if there exists an element $a \in M$ such that $N = Ra$, if $N$ is generated by one element: $$N = Ra = \{ra \mid r \in R\}.$$
We note these definitions do not require that the ring $R$ contain $1$, however this condition $A \subseteq RA$. We see that $RA$ is the smallest submodule of $M$ which contains $A$. 

**Prop:** Let $I$ be a left ideal of $R$, and $M$ is an $R$-module. Then $M$ is an $I$-module, and $IM$ is a submodule of the $R$-module $M$. 

**Def:** Let $A$ and $B$ be submodules of the $R$-module $M$. The *sum* of $A$ and $B$ is the set $$A + B := \{a+b \mid a \in A, b \in B\}.$$This is the smallest submodule which contains both $A$ and $B$.

In particular $N_1, \dots, N_n$ of $M$ $\sum_{i= 1}^n N_i$ is just the submodule generated by $\bigcup_{i = 1}^n N_i$ and is the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $N_i$, for all $i$. If $N_1, \dots, N_n$ are generated by the sets $A_1, \dots, A_n$, respectively, then $\sum_{i = 1}^n N_i$ is generated by $\bigcup_{i = 1}^n A_i$. 

**Def:** A submodule $N$ of an $R$-module $M$ may have many different generating sets. If $N$ is finitely generated, then there is a smallest nonnegative integer $d$ such that $N$ is generated by $d$ elements (and no fewer). Any generating set consisting of $d$ elements will be called a *minimal set of generators for $N$*. 

**Def:** Let $M_1, \dots, M_k$ be $R$-modules. The collection of $k$-tuples $(m_1, \dots, m_k)$ where $m_i \in M_i$ with addition and action of $R$ componentwise is called the *direct product* of $M_1, \dots, M_k$, denoted as $M_1 \times M_2 \times \dots \times M_k = \prod_{i = 1}^k M_i$. The direct product of $M_1, \dots M_k$ is also referred as the *direct sum* of $M_1, \dots, M_k$ and denoted $M_1 \oplus \dots M_k = \bigoplus_{i = 1}^k M_i$. 

**Def:** Let $\kappa$ be a nonzero cardinal and for each $\alpha  < \kappa$ let $M_\alpha$ be an $R$-module. 
- The *direct product* of the modules $M_\alpha$ is defined to be their product as abelian groups with the the action of $R$ componentwise multiplication. Denoted as $\prod_{\alpha < \kappa} M_\alpha$. 
- The *direct sum* of the modules $M_\alpha$ is defined to be the restricted direct product of the abelian groups with the action of $R$ componentwise multiplication. In other words consists of elements $\prod_{\alpha < \kappa}m_\alpha$ such that only finitely many components of $m_i$ are nonzero. The direct sum is denoted as $\bigoplus_{\alpha < \kappa} M_\alpha$. 

**Prop:** Let $N_1, \dots, N_k$ be submodules of the $R$-module $M$. Then the following are equivalent:
- The map $\pi: \prod_{i = 1}^k N_i \to \sum_{i = 1}^k N_i$ defined by $$\pi(a_1, \dots, a_k) = a_1 + \dots + a_k$$is an isomorphism (or $R$-modules): $\prod_{i = 1}^k N_i \cong \sum_{i = 1}^k N_i$. 
- $N_j \cap (N_1 + N_{j-1} + N_{j+1} + \dots N_k) = 0$ for all $j \in \{1, 2, \dots, k\}$. 
- For every $x \in \sum_{i = 1}^k N_i$ can be written *uniquely* in the form $a_1 + \dots + a_k$ with $a_i \in N_i$. 

**Def:** If an $R$-module $M = \sum_{i = 1}^k N_i$ is the sum of submodules $N_1, \dots, N_k$ of $M$ satisfying the equivalent conditions of the proposition above, then $M$ is said to be the *internal direct sum* of $N_1, \dots, N_k$, written: $$M = N_1 \oplus N_2 \oplus \dots \oplus N_k = \bigoplus_{i = 1}^k N_i.$$By the same proposition above, we see that the internal direct sum is isomorphic to the direct sum, and this is why they have the same notation.

**Def:** An $R$-module $F$ is said to be *free* on the subset $A$ of $F$ if for every nonzero element of $x$ of $F$, there exist unique nonzero element $r_1, \dots, r_n$ of $R$ and unique $a_1, \dots, a_n$ in $A$ such that $x = r_1 a_1 + \dots r_n a_n$ for some $n \in \Bbb N^+$. In this situation we say $A$ is *basis* or the *set of free generators* for $F$. If $R$ is commutative ring the cardinality of $A$ is called the *rank* of $F$.

**Th:** For any set $A$ there is a free $R$-module $R\langle A \rangle$ on the set $A$ and $R\langle A \rangle$ satisfies the following *universal property*: if $M$ is any $R$-module and $\varphi: A \to M$ is any map of sets, then there is a unique $R$-module homomorphism $\Phi: R\langle A\rangle \to M$ such that $\Phi(a) = \varphi(a)$, for all $a \in A$, that is, the following diagram commutes

```tikz
\usepackage{tikz-cd} 
\begin{document} 
\begin{tikzcd}     
A \arrow[dr, "\phi"] \arrow[r, hook]& R\langle A\rangle \arrow[d, dashed, "\Phi"]\\
& \mathrm{Im}(\phi)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{document}
```
Then $$R\langle A\rangle = \bigoplus_{a \in A} Ra.$$
**Cor:**
- If $F_1$ and $F_2$ are free modules on the same set $A$, there is a unique isomorphism between $F_1$ and $F_2$ which is the identity map on $A$.
- If $F$ is any free $R$-module with basis $A$, then $F \cong R\langle A\rangle$. In particular, $F$ enjoys the same universal property with respect to $A$ and $R\langle A \rangle$ does in the theorem above.

**Cor:** $R^n = R^m$ iff $n = m$.
