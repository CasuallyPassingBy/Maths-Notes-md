**Def:** A topological space $X$ is *zero-dimensional* if it has a base consisting of clopen sets. 

**Def:** A completely regular space $X$ is said to be *strongly zero-dimensional* if whenever $A$ and $B$ are disjoint zero sets, there is a clopen set $U$ with $A \subseteq U$ and $B\subseteq X\setminus U$.

**Obs:** Every subspace of a zero-dimensional space is zero-dimensional. The topological sum of zero-dimensional spaces is zero-dimensional, and the product of zero-dimensional spaces is zero-dimensional.

Note that the spaces $[0, \omega)^\omega$ and $D(2)^\omega$ are zero-dimensional spaces.
