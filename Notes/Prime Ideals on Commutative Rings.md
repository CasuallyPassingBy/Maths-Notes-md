**Def:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. An ideal $P$ is called a *prime ideal* if $P \neq R$ and whenever the product $ab$ of two elements $a, b\in R$ is an element of $P$, then at least one of $a$ and $b$ is an element of $P$. 

We are generalising the notion of [[Prime Numbers|prime]] in the integers. The *prime ideals of $\Bbb Z$ are just the ideals $p\Bbb Z$ of $\Bbb Z$ generated by prime numbers $p$ together with the ideal $0$.*

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Then the ideal $P$ is a prime ideal in $R$ iff the quotient ring $R/P$ is an integral domain. 

**Cor:** Let $R$ be commutative ring. Every maximal ideal of $R$ is a prime ideal. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. $P$ is a prime ideal of $R$ and $P$ contains no zero divisors then $R$ is an integral domain, 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. if $I$ and $J$ are ideals of $R$ and $P$ is a prime ideal of $R$ that contains $IJ$, then either $I$ or $J$ is contained in $P$. 

**Prop:** Let $\varphi:R \to S$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings.
- If $P$ is a prime ideal of $S$, the $\varphi^{-1}[P] = R$ or $\varphi^{-1}[R]$ is a prime ideal of $R$. In particular, if $R\subseteq S$, and $\varphi$ is the inclusion then $P \cap R$ is either $R$ or a prime ideal of $R$. 
- If $M$ is a maximal ideal of $S$ and $\varphi$ is surjective then $\varphi^{-1}[M]$ is a maximal ideal of $R$. 

**Prop:** If $R$ is a finite commutative ring, then every prime ideal of $R$ is maximal. 

**Prop:** In a Boolean ring every prime ideal is a maximal ideal. 

**Prop:** If $R$ is a commutative ring, then every prime ideal contains every nilpotent element. 

**Cor:** The [[Ring Ideals and Quotient Rings#Common Ideals|nilradical]] of a commutative ring $R$ is contained in the intersection of all prime ideals, i.e., $$\text{nil}(R) \subseteq \bigcap_{\substack{P \le R \\  P \text{ prime}} }P$$
**Prop:** If $R$ be a commutative ring, then every prime ideal of $R$ is a [[Ring Ideals and Quotient Rings#Common Ideals|radical ideal]]. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. The set of prime ideals in $R$ has a minimal element with respect to the inclusion (possibly the zero ideal). 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. The following statements are equivalent. 
- $R$ has exactly one prime ideal.
- Every element of $R$ is either nilpotent or an unit. 
- $R/(\text{nil }R)$ is a field. 

**Def:** A proper ideal $Q$ of a commutative ring $R$ is called *primary* if whenever $ab\in Q$ and $a\notin Q$ then there's $n \in \Bbb N$ such that $b^n \in Q$. 

**Obs:** Every prime ideal of $R$ is a primary ideal. The primary ideals of $\Bbb Z$ are $0$ and $(p^n)$, where $p$ is prime and $n$ is a positive integer.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring.
- An ideal $Q$ of $R$ is primary iff every zero divisor in $R/Q$ is a nilpotent element of $R/Q$. 
- If $Q$ is a primary ideal then $\text{rad }Q$ is a prime ideal.
