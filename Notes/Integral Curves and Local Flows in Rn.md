We have seen that $X \in \mathfrak X(U)$ in two ways: as a section of the tangent bundle and as a derivation of the algebra $\mathcal C^\infty (U)$. There's a third, having that $X$ is equivalent to a system of ordinary differential equations on $M$. 

**Def:** Let $s:(a, b) \to U$ be smooth. the velocity vector of $s$ at $t_0 \in (a, b)$ is: $$s'(t_0) = ds_{t_0}\left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\rvert_{t_0}\right) \in T_{s(t_0)}(M)$$just to be extra clear, $ds_{t_0}$ refers to the differential of $s$ at $t_0$.

**Def:** The map $s':(a,b) \to TU$ is called the velocity field of the smooth curve.

**Obs:** Let $\pi: TM \to M$, be the projection then $\pi \circ s' = s$, and that $s'$ is smooth.

**Def:** Let $X\in \mathfrak X(U)$ and $p \in M$. An integral curve to $X$ through $p$ is a smooth curve $s: (-\delta, \varepsilon) \to M$, defined for suitable $\delta, \varepsilon>0$, such that $s(0) = p$ and $s'(t) = X_{s(t)}$ for all $t\in (-\delta, \varepsilon)$.

Suppose that $s$ is an integral curve to $X \in \mathfrak X(M)$ through $x_0 \in U$ and $(U, x^1, \dots, x^n)$ a coordinate neighbourhood. Then $$ X = \sum f^i \frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}$$and $$s(t) = (x^1(t), \dots, x(t))$$we see that $$ds_t \left(\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\rvert_t\right) = \sum_{i = 1}^n \frac{dx^i}{dt}(t) \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right\rvert_t \in T_{s(t)}(U),$$but $$X_{s(t)} = \sum_{i = 1}^n f^i(x^1(t), \dots, x^n(t))\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x^i}\right\rvert_t$$
Thus $s$ is an integral curve of $X$ iff $$\frac{dx^i}{dt} = f^i(x^1(t), \dots, x^n(t))$$for $t$ in the domain of $s$ and $1 \le i \le n$. This is an (autonomous) system of ODE with solution subject to the initial condition $s(0) = x_0$. 

From [[Existence and Uniqueness of Solutions to Systems of Differential Equations|this]] existence and uniqueness theorem. we get that:

**Cor:** If $X \in \mathfrak X(U)$ and $x\in U$, then there is an integral curve to $X$ through $x$. Any two such curves agree on their common domains.

**Def:** A local flow $\Phi$ around $x_0 \in U$ is a smooth map $\Phi: (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times W \to U$ (written $\Phi(t, x) = \Phi(t, x)$) where $W$ is a suitable open neighbourhood of $x_0$ in $U$, such that:
- $\Phi_0: W \to U$ is an inclusion
- $\Phi_{t_1+t_2} = \Phi_{t_1} \circ \Phi_{t_2}$ whenever both sides of the equation are defined. 
If $z \in U$, the flow line through $z$ is the curve $\sigma(t) = \Phi_t(z)$, $-\varepsilon < t <\varepsilon$.

**Th:** Let $X \in \mathfrak X(U)$ and $x_0\in U$. Then there is a local flow around $x_0$ such that the flow lines are integral curves to $X$. Two such such local flows agree on their common domain.

**Def:** The local flow $\Phi$ associated to $X \in \mathfrak X(U)$ as in the theorem above is said to be generated by $X$. Also, the vector field $X$ is called the infinitesimal generator of the local flow $\Phi$. 

**Prop:** Let $X \in \mathfrak X(U)$ generate a local flow $\Phi:(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \times W \to U$. Then for each $q \in W$, $$d(\Phi_t)_q(X_q) = X_{\Phi_t(q)}, \qquad -\varepsilon < t <\varepsilon.$$

# The Lie Derivative of a Differential Form

**Def:** For $X$ a smooth vector field and $\omega$ a smooth $k$-form on a manifold $M$, the *Lie derivative* $\mathcal L_X \omega$ at $p \in M$ is $$(\mathcal L_X \omega)_p := \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{(\varphi_t^* \omega)_p - \omega_p}{t} = \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right\rvert_{t = 0} (\varphi^*_t \omega)_p$$
**Prop:** If $f$ is a smooth function and $X$ a smooth vector field on $M$, then $\mathcal L_X f = Xf$.
