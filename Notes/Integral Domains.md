**Def:** Let $R$ be a ring. $R$ is called an *integral domain* if $R$ is a unital commutative ring without zero divisors.

**Def:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $a, b\in R$ with $b\ne 0$.
- $a$ is said to be a *multiple* of $b$ if there exists an element $x\in R$ with $a = bx$. In this case $b$ is said to *divide* $a$ or be a *divisor* of $a$, written $b\mid a$.
- A *greatest common divisor* of $a$ and $b$ is nonzero element $d$ such that:
	- $d\mid a$ and $d\mid b$
	- if $d'\mid a$ and $d'\mid b$, then $d'\mid d$
	The greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$ will be denoted by $\text{gcd}(a,b)$, or by abusing notation, simple $(a,b)$. 

**Obs:** Note that $b\mid a$ in a ring $R$ iff $a\in (b)$ iff $(a)\subseteq (b)$. In particular, if $d$ is any divisor of both $a$ and $b$, then $(d)$ must contain both $a$ and $b$ and hence contain the ideal generated by $a$ and $b$. If $I$ is an ideal of $R$ generated by $a$ and $b$, then $d$ is a greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$ if
- $I$ is contained in the principal ideal $(d)$, and
- If $(d')$ is any principal ideal containing $I$, then $(d) \subseteq (d')$. 

**Prop:** If $a$ and $b$ are nonzero elements in the commutative ring $R$ such that the ideal generated by $a$ and $b$ is a principal ideal $(d)$, then $d$ is the greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. If two elements of $d$ and $d'$ of $R$ generate the same principal ideal, then $d' = ud$ for some unit $u$ in $R$. In particular, if $d$ and $d'$ are both greatest state common divisors of $a$ and $b$, then $d' = ud$ for some unit $u$. 

**Obs:** Note that $\mid$ is a [[Pre-orderings|pre-order]] on $R$. Then, the natural equivalence relation on $R$ induced by the preorder has equivalence class of $a$ is $\{au \in R \mid u \in R^\times\}$. 

**Def:** The ideals $A$ and $B$ of the ring $R$ are said to be *comaximal* if $A+ B =R$.

**Def:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1$ and let $a$ and $b$ nonzero elements of $R$. A *least common multiple* of $a$ and $b$ is an element $e\in R$ such that:
- $a\mid e$ and $b\mid e$, and
- if $a\mid e'$ and $b\mid e'$, then $e\mid e'$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring. The least common multiple of $a$ and $b$ is a generator for the unique largest principal ideal contained in $(a) \cap (b)$.

**Def:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. 
- Suppose $r\in R$ is nonzero and is not a unit. Then $r$ is called *irreducible* in $R$ if whenever $r=ab$ with $a,b\in R$, at least one of $a$ or $b$ be a unit in $R$. Otherwise, $r$ is said to be *reducible.*
- The nonzero element $p\in R$ is called *prime* in $R$ if the ideal $(p)$ generated by $p$ is a prime ideal. In other words, a nonzero element $p$ is a prime iff it is not a unit and whenever $p\mid ab$ for any $a, b\in R$, then either $p\mid a$ and $p\mid b$. 
- Two elements $a$ and $b$ of $R$ differing by a unit are said to be *associate* in $R$, i.e., $a = ub$ for some $u\in R^\times.$
