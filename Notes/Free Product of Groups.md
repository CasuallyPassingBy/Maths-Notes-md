Let $(G_\alpha)_{\alpha < \kappa}$ be an indexed family of groups. 

**Def:** A word in $(G_\alpha)_{\alpha < \kappa}$ is a finite sequence of any length $m \ge 0$ of elements of the disjoint union $\coprod_{\alpha< \kappa} G_\alpha$. The sequence of length zero, called the *empty word*, is denoted by $()$. Let $\cal W$ denote the set of all words in $(G_\alpha)$. We denote the identity elemento of $G_\alpha$ by $e_\alpha$.

We define an operation in $\cal W$ by concatenation:$$(g_1,\cdots, g_m)\cdot (h_1,\cdots, h_k) := g_1,\cdots, g_m, h_1, \cdots, h_k.$$Clearly, this multiplication is associative, and has the empty word as a two sided identity element. An elementary *reduction* is an operation of the following forms:
- $(g_1\cdots g_i, g_{i+1},\dots g_m) \mapsto (g_1\cdots g_i g_{i+1},\dots g_m)$ if $g_i, g_{i+1}\in G_\alpha$.
- $(g_1, \dots, g_{i-1}, e_\alpha, g_{i+1}, \dots, g_m) \mapsto (g_1, \dots, g_{i-1},  g_{i+1}, \dots, g_m)$.

Let $\sim$ denote the equivalence relation on words generated by elementary reductions: this means that $W \sim W'$ iff there a finite sequence of words $W = W_0, W_1, \dots, W_n= W'$ such that for each $i\in \{1,\dots, n\}$, either $W_i$ is obtained from $W_{i-1}$ by elementary reduction, or vice versa. 

The set of equivalence classes is called the *free product* of the groups $(G_\alpha)$, and is denoted by $\ast_{\alpha} G_\alpha$ or $G_1 *G_2\dots*G_n$. 

**Prop:** Given an indexed family of groups $(G_\alpha)_{\alpha <\kappa}$ their free product is the a group under multiplication operation induced by multiplication of words.

We say that a word $(g_1, \dots, g_m)$ is *reduced* if it cannot be shortened by elementary reduction. 

**Prop:** Every element of $*_\alpha G_\alpha$ is represented by a unique reduced word. 

Let us observe that for each group $G_\alpha$, there is a canonical map $\iota_\alpha: G_\alpha \hookrightarrow \ast_\alpha G_\alpha$, defined by sending $g\in G_\alpha$ to the equivalence class of the word $(g)$. Each of this maps are injective homomorphisms. We identify the image of $\iota_\alpha$ with $G_\alpha$, and the word $(g_1, \dots, g_m)$ with $g_1\cdots g_m$. 

**Characteristic Property of the Free Product:** Let $(G_\alpha)_{\alpha \in< \kappa}$ be an indexed family of groups. For any group $H$ and any collection of homomorphisms $\varphi_\alpha: G_\alpha \to H$, there exists a unique homomorphism $\Phi: *_\alpha G_\alpha \to H$ such that for each $\alpha$ the diagram commutes

**Cor:** The free product is the coproduct in the category of groups

Because of this, I am going to denote it as $\coprod_\alpha G_\alpha$. 

**Cor:** The free product is the unique group (up to isomorphism) satisfying the characteristic property.

# Amalgamated Free Product

**Def:** Let $H$, $G_1$, $G_2$ be groups, and $f_1: H \to G_1$ and $f_2: H \to G_2$ group homomorphisms. The *amalgamated free product of $G_1$ and $G_2$ along $H$*, denoted by $G_1 \ast_H G_2$, is the quotient group $G_1 \ast G_2/\overline C$, where $C := \{f_1(h) f_2(h)^{-1} \mid h \in H\}$.

**Presentation of an Amalgamated Free Product:** Let $f_1: H\to G_1$ and $f_2 : H \to G_2$ be group homomorphisms. Suppose $G_1, G_2$ and $H$ have following presentations: $$\begin{align*} G_1 &\cong \langle \alpha_1,\dots, \alpha_m \mid \rho_1,\dots, \rho_r\rangle, \\
G_2 &\cong \langle \beta_1,\dots, \beta_n \mid \sigma_1,\dots, \sigma_s\rangle, \\
H &\cong \langle \gamma_1,\dots, \gamma_p \mid \tau_1,\dots, \tau_t\rangle.
\end{align*}$$
Then the amalgamated free product has the presentation $$G_1 \ast_H G_2 \cong \langle a_1, \dots, \alpha_m, \beta_1,\dots, \beta_n \mid \rho_1, \dots, \rho_r,\sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_s,u_1 = v_1,\dots,u_p = v_p\rangle, $$where $u_a$ is an expression for $f_1(\gamma_a)\in G_1$ in terms of the generators, and $v_a$ similarly, expresses $f_2(\gamma_a)\in G_2$ in terms of its generators.
