Throughout this note $R$ is a unital ring.

**Def:** Let $A$ be any subset of $R$. 
- Let $(A)$ denote the smallest ideal of $R$ containing $A$, called the *ideal generated by $A$*.
- Let $RA$ denote the set of all finite sums of the form $ra$ with $r\in R$ and $a\in A$,i.e., $$RA:= \{r_1a_1+r_2a_2+\dots +r_n a_n \mid r_i\in R, a_i \in A, n \in \Bbb N\}, $$where $RA = 0$ when $A = \varnothing$. Similarly, $$AR := \{a_1r_1+\dots+a_nr_n \mid r_i \in R, a_i \in A, n \in \Bbb N\},$$and $$RAR := \{r_1a_1r'_1+\dots+r_na_nr_n'\mid r_i, r_i'\in R, a_i\in A, n \in \Bbb N\}. $$
- An ideal generated by single element is called a *principal ideal*.
- An ideal generated by a finite set is called a *finitely generated ideal*.

The idea of a generated ideal mimics the idea of the generated subgroup. Then we see that $$(A) := \bigcap_{\substack{I \le R \\ A\subseteq I}} I,$$ $(A)$ is the intersection of all ideals of $R$ that contain the set $A$.

The *left ideal generated by $A$* is the intersection of all left ideals of $R$ that contain $A$. This left ideal is obtained from $A$ by closing $A$ under all the operations that define a left ideal. We can reason that $RA$ *is precisely the left ideal generated by $A$*. Similarly, $AR$ *is the right ideal generated by $A$*, and $RAR$ *is the two-sided ideal generated by $A$.*

**Obs:** If $R$ is commutative, then $RA = AR = RAR = (A)$. 

Notice that the element $b\in R$ belongs to the ideal $(a)$ iff $b = ra$ for some $r\in R$, i.e., iff $b$ is a *multiple* of $a$ or, put another way, $a$ *divides* $b$ in $R$. Also, $b\in (a)$ iff $(b)\subseteq (a)$. 

**Def:** Let $a, b\in R$, we say that $b$ *divides* $a$ if there exists $r\in R$ such that $b = ra$, we denote this by relation by $b \mid a$. The following are equivalent ways, of writing the same relation:
- $b\mid a$
- $b\in (a)$
- $(b)\subseteq (a)$ 

**Obs:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. $(a) = (b)$ for some elements $a, b\in R$ iff there's a unit $u \in R$ such that $a=ub$. 

**Prop:** Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$.
- $I = R$ iff $I$ contains a unit.
- Assume $R$ is commutative. Then $R$ is a field iff  its only ideals are $0$ and $R$. 

**Prop:** $R$ is a skew-field iff its only left/right ideals are $0$ and $R$. In particular, are simple fields. 

**Cor:** If $R$ is a field then any nonzero ring homomorphism from $R$ into another ring is an injection. 

**Prop:** In a Boolean ring every finitely generated ideal is principal. 

**Prop:** If $R$ is commutative ring and $N = (a_1, \dots, a_m)$ where each $a_i \in \text{nil}(R)$, then $N$ is a [[Ring Ideals and Quotient Rings|nilpotent]] ideal. In particular, if $\text{nil}(R)$ is finitely generated, then $\text{nil}(R)$ is nilpotent.
