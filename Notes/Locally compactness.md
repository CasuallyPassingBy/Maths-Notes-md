**Def:** A topological space $X$ is said to be *locally compact* if for every $p\in x$ there is a compact subset of $X$ containing a neighbourhood of $p$. 

**Prop:** Let $X$ be a $T_2$ space. The following are equivalent.
- $X$ is locally compact.
- Each point of $X$ has a precompact neighbourhood.
- $X$ has a basis of precompact open subsets. 

**Prop:** Every manifold with or without boundary is locally compact. 

**Prop:** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space, then it is Tychonoff.

**Prop:** Any open or closed subset of a locally compact $T_2$ space is locally compact $T_2$ space.

**Baire Category Theorem:** Every locally compact $T_2$ space is a Baire space

**Lemma:** Suppose $q:X \to Y$ is a quotient map and $K$ is a locally compact $T_2$ space. Then the product map $q\times \text{id}_K: X \times K \to Y \times K$ is a quotient map.

**Prop:** A second countable, locally compact Hausdorff space admits an exhaustion by compact sets.

**Prop:** A second countable, locally compact $T_2$ space is $\sigma$[[σ-Local Compactness|-locally compact]]. 

**Paracompactness Theorem:** Every second countable, locally compact Hausdorff space is [[Paracompacteness|paracompact]].

**Cor:** Every topological manifold is paracompact.
