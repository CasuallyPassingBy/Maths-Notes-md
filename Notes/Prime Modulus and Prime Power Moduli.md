# Prime Power Moduli

To solve a polynomial congruence $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod{p^k}$, we start with a solution modulo $p$, then move on to the modulo $p^2$, then to $p^3$, and by iteration to $p ^k$. Suppose that $x = a$ is a solution of $f(x) \equiv 0 \pmod {p^j}$ and we want to use it to get a solution modulo $p^{j+1}$. The idea is to try to get a solution $x = a +tp^j$, where $t$ is to be determined, by use of Taylor's expansion $$f(a+tp^j) = \sum_{k = 0}^n \frac{(tp^j)^k f^{(k)}(a)}{k!} $$where $n$ is the presumed degree of the polynomial $f(x)$. All derivatives beyond the $n$th are identically zero. Now with respect to the modulus $p^{j+1}$, then $$f(a+tp^j) \equiv f(a) + tp^jf'(a) \pmod {p^{j+1}}$$
The congruence above reveals how $t$ should be chosen if $x = a+tp^j$ is to be be a solution of $f(x) \equiv0\pmod {p^{j+1}}$. Then we get that  $$tf'(a) \equiv -\frac{f(a)}{p^j} \pmod p$$is a linear congruence in $t$. This congruence may have no solution, one solution, or $p$ solutions. If $f'(a) \not\equiv 0\pmod p$, then this congruence has exactly one solution, and we obtain

**Hensel's Lemma:** Suppose that $f(x)$ is a polynomial with integral coefficients. If $f(a) \equiv 0\pmod{p^j}$ and $f'(a) \not\equiv\pmod p$, then there is a unique $t\pmod p$ such that $f(a+tp^j) \equiv 0\pmod{p^{j+1}}$. 

If $f(a) \equiv  0\pmod{p^j}$, $f(b) \equiv 0\pmod{p^k}$, $j <k$, and $a\equiv b \pmod {p^j}$, then we say that $b$ *lies above* $a$ or $a$ *lifts to* $b$. If $f(a) \equiv 0\pmod{p^j}$, then the root $a$ is called *nonsingular* if $f'(a) \not\equiv 0\pmod p$; otherwise it is *singular*. 

By Hensel's lemma we see that a nonsingular root $a \pmod p$ lifts to a unique root $a_2\pmod {p^2}$. Since $a_2\equiv a\pmod p$, we see that $f'(a_2) \equiv f'(a)\not\equiv 0 \pmod p$. By a second application of Hensel's lemma we may lift $a_2$ to form a root $a_3$ of $f(x)$ modulo $p^3$, and so on. In general, we find that a nonsingular a root $a$ modulo $p$ lifts to a unique root $a_j$ modulo $p^j$ for $j \ge 2$. We see that this sequence is generated by means of the recursion $$a_{j+1} = a_j - f(a_j) \overline {f'(a)}$$where $\overline{f'(a)}$ is the multiplicative inverse modulo $p$. 

**Th:** Let $f(x)$ be a polynomial with integral coefficients. Suppose that $f(a) \equiv 0\pmod{p^j}$, that $p^\tau\;\|\; f'(a)$ and that $j \ge 2\tau +1$. If $b \equiv a\pmod{p^{j-\tau}}$ then $f(b)\equiv f(a) \pmod{p^j}$ and $p^\tau\;\|\; f'(b)$. Moreover, there is a unique $t\pmod p$ such that $f(a+tp^{j-\tau})\equiv 0 \pmod {p^{j+1}}$. 

**Cor:** Suppose that $f(a) \equiv 0\pmod{p^j}$ and that $f'(a) \not\equiv 0 \pmod p$. Let $\overline{f'(a)}$ be an integer chosen so that $f'(a)\overline{f'(a)} \equiv 1\pmod{p^j}$, and put $b = a- f(a)\overline{f'(a)}$, then $f(b) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^{2j}}$. 

**Prop:** Let $f(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ be a polynomial integral coefficients with $n$-variables. Suppose that $f(a_1,\dots, a_n) \equiv 0\pmod p$, and that $\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_ j} f|_{(a_1,\dots, a_n)} \not\equiv 0\pmod p$ for at least one $j$. Them the congruence $f(x_1, \dots, x_n) \equiv 0 \pmod{p^j}$ has a solution for every $j$. 

# Prime Modulus

**Th:** If the degree $n$ of $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$ is greater or equal to $p$, then either every integers is a solution of $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$ or there is a monic polynomial $g(x)$ having integral coefficient $1$, such that $g(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$ is of degree less than $p$ and the solutions of $g(x)\equiv 0\pmod p$ are precisely those of $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$. 

**Th:** The congruence $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$ of degree $n$ has at most $n$ solutions. 

**Cor:** If $b_n x^n + b_{n-1}x^{n-1}+ \dots+b_0 \equiv 0\pmod p$ has more than $n$ solutions, then all the coefficients $b_i$ are divisible by $p$.

**Th:** If $F(x)$ is a function that maps residue classes $\pmod p$ to residue classes $\pmod p$, then there is a polynomial $f(x)$ with integral coefficients and degree at most $p-1$ such that $F(x) \equiv f(x)\pmod p$ for all residue classes $x\pmod p$. 

**Th:** The congruence $f(x) \equiv 0\pmod p$ of degree $n$, with leading coefficient $a_n = 1$, has $n$ solutions iff $f(x)$ is a factor of $x^p-x$ modulo $p$, that is, iff $x^p-x = f(x)q(x) +ps(x)$, where $q(x)$ and $s(x)$ have integral coefficients, $q(x)$ is monic and has degree $p-n$, and where $s(x)$ is a polynomial of degree less than $n$ or $s(x)$ is zero.

**Cor:** If $d\mid p-1$, then $x^d \equiv 1\pmod p$ has $d$ solutions.
