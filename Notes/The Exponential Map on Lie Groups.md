# One-Parameter Subgroups

**Def:** A *one-parameter subgroup of $G$* is defined to be a Lie group homomorphism $\gamma: \Bbb R \to G$, with $\Bbb R$ considered as Lie group under addition. By this definition, a one-parameter subgroup is *not* a Lie subgroup of $G$, but rather a homomorphism into $G$.

**Prop:** The image of a one-parameter subgroup is a Lie subgroup when endowed with a suitable smooth manifold structure. 

**Characterisation of One-Parameter Subgroup:** Let $G$ be a Lie group. The one-parameter subgroups of $G$ are precisely the maximal integral curves of left-invariante vector fields starting at the identity,

Given $X\in \text{Lie}(G)$, the one parameter subgroup determined by $X$ in this ways is called the *one-parameter subgroup generated by $X$*. Because left-invariant vector fields are uniquely determined by their values at the identity, it follows that each one-parameter subgroup is uniquely determined by its initial velocity in $T_e G$, and thus there are one-to-one correspondences $$\{\text{one-parameter subgroups of }G\} \longleftrightarrow \text{Lie}(G) \longleftrightarrow T_e G. $$
The one-parameter subgroups of $\text{GL}(n, \Bbb R)$ are not hard to compute explicitly. 

**Prop:** For any $A \in {\frak gl}(n,\Bbb R)$, let $$e^A := \sum_{k = 0}^\infty \frac1{k!} A^k.  $$This series converges to a invertible matrix $e^A\in \text{GL}(n, \Bbb R)$, and the one-parameter subgroup of $\text{GL}(n,\Bbb R)$ generated by $A \in {\frak gl}(n,\Bbb R)$ is $\gamma(t) = e^{tA}$. We can usually calculate this series by diagonalizing or block diagonalizing, more of that [[Matrix Functions#Matrix Exponential|here]]. 

**Prop:** Suppose $G$ is a Lie group and $H \subseteq G$ is a Lie subgroup. The one-parameter subgroups of $H$ are precisely those one-parameters subgroups of $G$ whose initial velocities lie in $T_e H$. 

# The Exponential Map

**Def:** Given a Lie group $G$ with Lie algebra $\frak g$, we define a map $\exp: \mathfrak g \to G$, called the *exponential map of $G$*, as follows: for any $X\in \frak g$, we set $$\exp(X) := \gamma(1),  $$where $\gamma$ is the one-parameter subgroup generated by $X$, or equivalently the integral curve of $X$ starting at the identity.

**Prop:** Let $G$ be a Lie group. For any $X\in \text{Lie}(G)$, $\gamma(s) = \exp(sX)$ is the one-parameter subgroup of $G$ generated by $X$.

We see that the exponential map of $\text{GL}(n,\Bbb R)$ is given by $\exp A = e^A$. This is the reason why we use the term *exponential map*. 

**Example:** If $V$ is a finite-dimensional real vector space, a choice of basis for $V$ yields an isomorphism $\text{GL}(V) \cong \text{GL}(n,\Bbb R)$ and ${\frak gl}(V) \cong {\frak gl}(n, \Bbb R)$. The analysis of $\text{GL}(n,\Bbb R)$ case shows that the exponential map of $\text{GL}(V)$ can be written in the form $$\exp A = \sum_{k = 0}^\infty \frac1{k!} A^k, $$where we consider $A\in {\frak gl}(V)$ as a linear map from $V$ to itself, and $A^k = A\circ \dots \circ A$ is the $k$-fold composition of $A$ with itself.

**Properties of the Exponential Map:** Let $G$ be a Lie group and let $\frak g$ be its Lie algebra.
- The exponential map is a smooth map from $\frak g$ to $G$.
- For any $X\in \frak g$ and $s, t\in \Bbb R$, $$\exp((s+t)X) = \exp(sX) \exp(tX).$$
- For any $X\in \frak g$, $$(\exp X)^{-1} = \exp(-X).$$
- For any $X \in \frak g$ and $n \in\Bbb Z$,  $$(\exp X)^n = \exp(n X). $$
- The differential $(d\exp)_0: T_0 {\frak g} \to T_e G$ is the identity map, under the canonical identifications of both $T_0 {\frak g}$ and $T_e G$ with $\frak g$ itself. 
- The exponential map restricts to a diffeomorphism from some neighbourhood of $0$ in $\frak G$ to a neighbourhood of $e$ in $G$.
- If $H$ is another Lie group, $\frak h$ is its Lie algebra, and $\Phi: G \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism, the following diagram commutes 

- the flow of $\theta$ of a left-invariant vector field $X$ is given by $\theta_t = R_{\exp (tX)}$, right multiplication by $\exp(tX)$. 

**Prop:** Let $G$ be a Lie group, and let $H \subseteq G$ be a Lie subgroup. With $\text{Lie}(H)$ considered as a subalgebra of $\text{Lie}(G)$ in the usual way, then exponential map of $H$ is the restriction to $\text{Lie}(H)$ of the exponential map of $G$, and  $$\text{Lie}(H) =\{X\in \text{Lie}(G) \mid \exp(t X) \in H \text{ for all }t\in \Bbb R\}. $$
# The Closed Subgroup Theorem

**Prop:** Let $G$ be a Lie group and let $\frak g$ be its Lie algebra. For any $X, Y\in \frak g$, there is a smooth function $Z: (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon) \to G$ for some $\varepsilon> 0$ such that the following identity holds for all $t\in (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$: $$(\exp(tX))(\exp(tY)) = \exp(t(X+Y)+ t^2Z(t)). $$
**Cor:** Let $G$ be a Lie group and let $\frak g$ be its Lie algebra. For any $X, Y\in \frak g$ the following equality is satisfied:$$\lim_{n \to\infty}\left(\left(\exp \frac tn X\right)\left(\exp \frac tn Y\right)\right) = \exp(t (X+Y)). $$

**Closed Subgroup Theorem:** Suppose $G$ is a Lie group and $H\subseteq G$ is a subgroup that is also a closed subset of $G$. Then $H$ is an embedded Lie subgroup. 

**Cor:** If $G$ is a Lie group and $H$ is any subgroup of $G$, then the following statements are equivalent.
- $H$ is closed in $G$.
- $H$ is an embedded submanifold of $G$.
- $H$ is an embedded Lie subgroup of $G$.
