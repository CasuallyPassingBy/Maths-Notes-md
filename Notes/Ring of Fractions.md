In this note $R$ is a commutative ring. 

**Th:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Let $D$ be any nonempty subset of $R$ that doesn't contain $0$, doesn't contain any zero divisors and is closed under multiplication (i.e., $ab\in D$ for all $a, b\in D$). Then there is a commutative ring $Q$ with $1$ such that $Q$Q contains $R$ as a subring and every element of $D$ is a unit in $Q$. The ring $Q$ has the following additional properties.
- Every element of $Q$ is of the form $rd^{-1}$ for some $r\in R$ and $d\in D$. In particular if $D = R\setminus \{0\}$, then $Q$ is a field.
- The ring $Q$ is the *smallest* ring containing $Q$ in which all elements of $D$ become units, in the following sense. Let $S$ be any commutative ring with identity and let $\varphi: R \to S$ be any injective ring homomorphism such that $\varphi(d)$ is a unit in $S$ for every $d\in D$. Then there is an injective homomorphism $\Phi: Q \to S$ such that $\Phi|_R= \varphi$. In other words, any ring containing an isomorphic copy of $R$ in which all the elements of $D$ become units must also contain an isomorphic copy of $Q$, and this diagram commutes

The construction is as follows. Let $\mathcal F = R \times D$ and define the relation $\sim$ on $\cal F$ by $$(r, d) \sim (s,e) \iff re = sd.$$
We see that $\sim$ is an equivalence relation. We denote the equivalence class of $(r,d)$ by $r/d.$$$\frac{r}{d} = \{(a,b) \in {\cal F}\mid rb= ad\}.$$
Let $Q$ be ${\cal F}/\sim$. Note that $r/d = (re)/(de)$ in $Q$ for all $e\in D$, since $D$ is closed under multiplication.

We now define an additive and multiplicative structure on $Q$: $$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} := \frac{ad+bc}{bd} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{a}{b}\times \frac{c}{d} := \frac{ac}{bc}.$$
We need to check the following things.
- the operations are well-defined. 
- $Q$ is an abelian group under addition, where the additive identity is $0/d$ for all $d\in D$, and the additive inverse $a/d$ is $(-a)/d$. 
- multiplication is associative, distributive and commutative, and
- $Q$ has identity, $d/d$ for any $d\in D$.

Next we embed $R$ into $Q$ by defining $\iota:R \to Q$ by $r\mapsto (rd)/d$ where $d$ is any element of $D$. 

Note that this construction is every similar the construction of the [[rational numbers]] from the [[integer numbers]], 

**Def:** Let $R$, $D$ and $Q$ be as in the theorem above.
- The ring $Q$ is called the *ring of fractions* of $D$ with respect to $R$ and is denoted $D^{-1}R$. 
- If $R$ is an integral domain and $D = R\setminus\{0\}$, $Q$ is called the *field of fractions* or *quotient field* of $R$.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain and let $D$ be a nonempty subset of $R$ that is closed under multiplication. The ring of fractions $D^{-1}R$ is isomorphic to a subring of the quotient field of $R$. 

**Cor:** Let $R$ be a integral domain and let $Q$ be the field of fraction of $R$. If a field $F$ contains a subring $R'$ isomorphic to $R$, then the subfield of $F$ generated by $R'$ is isomorphic to $Q$. 

**Cor:** Let $F$ be a field. $F$ contains a unique smallest subfield $F_0$ and that $F_0$ is isomorphic to either $\Bbb Q$ or $\Bbb Z/p\Bbb Z$ for some prime $p$. We call $F_0$ the *prime subfield* of $F$.
