Let $R$ be a ring.

# Construction

Let us consider the abelian group $$R' := \bigoplus_{n \in \Bbb N} R.$$This is the set of functions from $\Bbb N$ to $R$ with finite support. We would like to give this group a multiplication that is inspired by the multiplication on $R$. To do this, let $(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N}, (b_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} \in R'$, $$(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} * (b_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} := \left(\sum_{k = 0}^n a_k b_{n-k}\right)_{n \in \Bbb N}.$$This operation is the *discrete convolution* of the two sequences. We can check that is associative and distributes over the sum. This means that $R'$ is a ring. Just a couple of things we should keep in mind. If $R$ is commutative, then it is easy to check that so is $R'$. Lastly, if $R$ is unital, then $R'$ is also unital with the multiplicative identity being $$(1, 0, 0 \dots).$$
We usually don't write $R'$. The usual notation is $R[x]$, and we write the element $(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N}\in R'$ as $$\sum_{n = 0}^\infty a_n x^n,$$where $x$ is treated as a formal object. The elements of $R[x]$ are called *polynomials*. We call $R[x]$ the *ring of polynomials in the variable $x$ with coefficients in $R$*. 

# Properties

**Def:** Let $p(x) = \sum_{n = 0}^\infty a_n x^n$. We define the *degree of $p(x)$* to be $$\deg(p(x)) := \max\{n \in \Bbb N \mid a_n \neq  0\}. $$In this case, we call $a_n$ the *leading coefficient of $p(x)$* and $a_n x^n$ the *leading term of $p(x)$*, when $n = \deg(p(x))$. The polynomial is monic if their leading coefficient is $1$. We say that a polynomial is *constant* if their degree is $0$ 

The ring $R$ appears in $R[x]$ as the *constant polynomials*. Note that $R\not\subseteq R[x]$. This actually tells us that $R\hookrightarrow R[x]$ since there's an injective ring homomorphism. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring, and $p(x) = \sum_{k = 0}^\infty a_n x^n$
-  $p(x)$ is a unit iff $a_0$ is a unit in $R$ and $a_k$ is a zero divisor for $k \ge 1$. 
- $p(x)$ is nilpotent iff $a_k$ is nilpotent for $k \ge 0$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain and let $p(x), q(x) \in R[x]\setminus\{0\}$. Then:
- $\deg(p(x)q(x)) = \deg(p(x)) + \deg(p(x))$. 
- The units of $R[x]$ are just the units in $R$
- $R[x]$ is an integral domain. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a ring. We consider the evaluation function from $R[x]$ to $R$ to be $\text{eval}_a: R[x] \to R$ defined by $$p(x) = \sum_{n = 0}^\infty b_nx^n \mapsto b_n a^n,$$with $a\in R$. The evaluation function is a ring homomorphism iff $a\in Z(R)$. 

**Cor:** Let $R$ be a unital ring and $a, b\in R$. If $a\in Z(R)$, then $$(a+b)^n = \sum_{k = 0}^n {n \choose k} a^k b^{n-k},$$where ${n\choose k}$ is interpreted to be the $1+1+\dots + 1$ of the identity of $R$ taken ${n\choose k}$-times.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring. The principal ideal generated by $x$ in the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is a prime ideal iff $R$ is an integral domain. Additionally, $(x)$ is maximal ideal iff $R[x]$ is a field. 

**Prop:** Assume $R$ is commutative. Let $f(x)$ be a monic polynomial in $R[x]$ of degree $n \ge 1$. 
- Every element of $R[x]/(f(x))$ is of the form $\overline{p(x)}$ for some polynomial $p(x)\in R[x]$ of degree less than $n$, i.e., $$R[x]/(f(x)) =  \left\{\left.\sum_{k = 0}^{n-1} \overline{a_kx^k} \;\right\rvert\;\forall 0\le i <n(a_i \in R)\right\}.$$
- If $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are distinct polynomials in $R[x]$ which are both of degree less than $n$, then $\overline{p(x)} \neq \overline{q(x)}$. 
- If $f(x) = a(x)b(x)$  where both $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ have degree less than $n$, then $\overline{a(x)}$ and $\overline{b(x)}$ are zero divisors in $R[x]/(f(x))$. 

**Prop:** Let $I$ be an ideal of the ring $R$ and let $(I) = I[x]$ denote the ideal of $R[x]$ generated by $I$, the set of polynomials with coefficients in $I$. Then $$R[x]/I[x]  \cong (R/I)[x].$$

# Polynomials Rings Over Fields

**Th:** Let $F$ be a field. The polynomial ring $F[x]$ is an [[Euclidean Domains|Euclidean domain]]. Specifically, if $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ are two polynomials in $F[x]$ with $b(x)$ nonzero, then *unique* $q(x)$ and $r(x)$ in $F[x]$ such that $$a(x) = q(x) b(x)+r(x) \quad r(x) = 0 \text{ or } \deg(r(x))<\deg(b(x)).$$
**Cor:** If $F$ is a field, then $F[x]$ is a [[Principal Ideal Domains|principal ideal domain]] and a [[Unique Factorisation Domains|unique factorisation domain.]] 
**Cor:** Let $f(x)$ be a polynomial in $F[x]$. $F[x]/(f(x))$ is a field iff $f(x)$ is irreducible.

We note that the quotient and remainder in the Division algorithm applied to $a(x), b(x)\in F[x]$ are *independent of field extension* in the following sense. Suppose $F$ is a field contained in the field $E$ and $a(x) = Q(x) b(x) + R(Xx)$ from some $Q(x), R(x) \in E[x]$ that satisfy the conditions of the theorem above. We can also write $a(x) = q(x) b(x) + r(x)$ for some $q(x), r(x) \in F[x]$. By uniqueness, $q(x) = Q(x)$ and $r(x) = R(x)$. In particular, $b(x) \mid a(x)$ in the ring $E[x]$ iff $b(x) \mid a(x)$ in the ring $F[x]$. Also, the greatest common divisor of $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ is the same, once we make it unique by specifying it to be monic, whether these elements are viewed in $F[x]$ or in $E[x]$. 

**Prop:** Let $f(x) \in F[x]$ be a polynomial of degree $n \ge 1$. For each $\overline{g(x)}$ there is a unique polynomial $g_0(x) \in F[x]$ of degree $\le n-1$ such that $\overline{g(x)} = \overline{g_0(x)}$. Equivalently, the elements $\overline 1, \overline x, \dots,\overline{x^{n-1}}$ are a *basis* of the vectors space $F[x]/(f(x))$ over $F$. In particular, the dimension of this space is $n$. 

**Cor:** Let $F$ be a finite field of order $q$ and let $f(x)$ be a polynomial in $F[x]$ of degree $n \ge 1$, then $F[x]/(f(x))$ has $q^n$ elements.

**Prop:** If $F$ is a field, then $F[x]$ has infinitely many irreducible elements. 

**Prop:** Let $F$ be a field. The maximal ideal in $F[x]$ are the ideals $(f(x))$ generated by irreducible polynomials $f(x)$. In particular, $F[x]/(f(x))$ is a field iff $f(x)$ is irreducible. 

**Prop:** Let $g(x)$ be a nonconstant element of $F[x]$ and let$$g(x) = \prod_{k = 1}^m f_k(x)^{\alpha_k} $$be its factorisation into irreducibles, where $f_k(x)$ are distinct. Then we have the following isomorphisms of rings: $$F[x]/(g(x)) \cong \prod_{k = 1}^m F[x]/(f_k(x)^{\alpha_k}).$$
 This is actually a corollary of the [[Chinese remainder theorem for rings]]. 

**Prop:** If the polynomial $f(x)$ has roots $\alpha_1,\dots, \alpha_k$ in $F$ (not necessarily distinct), then $f(x)$ has $(x-\alpha_1)\cdots (x-\alpha_k)$ as a factor. In particular, a polynomial of degree $n$ in one variable over a field $F$ has at most $n$ roots in $F$, even counted with multiplicity.

**Prop:** A finite subgroup of the multiplicative group of a field is cyclic. In particular, if $F$ is a finite field, then the multiplicative group $F^\times$ of nonzero elements of $F$ is a cyclic group. 

**Cor:** Let $p$ be a prime. The multiplicative group $(\Bbb Z/p \Bbb Z)^\times$ of nonzero residue classes $\mod p$ is cyclic. 

**Cor:** Let $n \ge 2$ be an integer with factorisation $n = \prod_{k = 0}^\infty p_k ^{\alpha_k}$ in $\Bbb Z$. We have the following isomorphisms of multiplicative groups:
- $(\Bbb Z/n\Bbb Z)^\times \cong \prod_{k = 0}^\infty (\Bbb Z/p^\alpha \Bbb Z)^\times$.
- $(\Bbb Z/2^\alpha\Bbb Z)^\times$ is the product of a cyclic group of order $2$ and a cyclic group of order $2^{\alpha -2}$ for all $\alpha \ge 2$. 
- $(\Bbb Z/p^\alpha \Bbb Z)^\times$ is a cyclic group of order $p^\alpha(p-1)$, for all odd primes $p$. 

## Polynomial rings that Unique Factorisation Domain

**Gauss' Lemma:** Let $R$ be a unique factorisation domain with field of fractions $F$ and let $p(x)\in R[x]$. If $p(x)$ is reducible in $F[x]$ then $p(x)$ is reducible in $R[x]$. More precisely, if $p(x) = A(x)B(x)$ for some nonconstant polynomials $A(x), B(x)\in F[x]$, then there are nonzero elements $r,s\in F$ such that $rA(x) = a(x)$ and $sB(x) = b(x)$ both lie in $R[x]$ and $p(x) = a(x)b(x)$ is a factorisation in $R[x]$.

**Cor:** Let $R$ be a unique factorisation domain, let $F$ be its field of fractions and let $p(x)\in R[x]$. Suppose the greatest common divisor of the coefficients of $p(x)$ is $1$. Then $p(x)$ is the irreducible in $R[x]$ iff it is irreducible in $F[x]$. In particular, if $p(x)$ is a monic polynomial that is irreducible in $R[x]$, then $p(x)$ is irreducible in $F[x]$. 

**Th:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. $R$ is a unique factorisation domain iff $R[x]$ is a unique factorisation domain.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain with quotient field $F$ and let $p(x)$ be a monic polynomial in $R[x]$, If we assume $p(x) = a(x)b(x)$ where $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ are monic polynomials in $F[x]$ of smaller degree than $p(x)$, and $a(x)\notin R[x]$, then $R$ is not a unique factorisation domain. 

## Irreducibility Criteria

**Prop:** Let $F$ be a field and let $p(x)\in F[x]$. Then $p(x)$ has a fact of degree one iff $p(x)$ has a root in $F$, i.e., there is an $\alpha\in F$ with $p(\alpha) = 0$. 

**Cor:** A polynomial of degree two or three over a field $F$ is reducible only it has a root in $F$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a an integral domain, $F$ its field of fractions, and $p(x) = a_n x^n + \dots +a_0$ be a polynomial of degree $n$ with coefficients in $R$. If $r/s\in F$ with $(r)$ and $(s)$ are comaximal, and $r/s$ is a root of $p(x)$, then $r \mid a_0$ and $s\mid a_n$. In particular, if $p(x)$ is a monic polynomial with coefficients in $R$ and $p(d) \neq 0$ for all $d\in R$ such that $d\mid a_0$, the $p(x)$ has no roots in $F$. 

**Cor (Rational Root Theorem):** Let $p(x) = a_n x^n+ \dots + a_0$ be a polynomial of degree $n$ with integer coefficients. If $r/s\in \Bbb Q$ is in the lowest terms, then $r\mid a_0$ and $s\mid a_n$. In particular, if $p(x)$ is a monic polynomial with integer coefficients and $p(d) \neq 0$ for all integers $d$ dividing the constant term of $p(x)$, then $p(x)$ has no roots in $\Bbb Q$. 

**Prop:** Let $I$ be a proper ideal in the integral domain $R$ and let $p(x)$ be a nonconstant monic polynomial in $R[x]$. If the image of $p(x)$ in $(R/I)[x]$ cannot be factored in $(R/I)[x]$ into two polynomials of smaller degree, then $p(x)$ is irreducible in $R[x]$. 

**Eisenstein's Criterion for Integral Domains:** Let $P$ be a prime ideal of the integral domain $R$ and let $f(x) = x^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+ \dots + x_n$ be a polynomial in $R[x]$, with $n\ge 1$. Suppose that $a_k \in P$ for $0\le k < n$ and $a_0 \notin P^2$, then $f(x)$ is irreducible in $R[x]$. 

**Eisenstein's Criterion for Unique Factorisation Domains:** Let $P$ be a prime ideal of the unique factorisation domain $R$ and let $f(x) = a_nx^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+ \dots + x_n$ be a polynomial in $R[x]$, with $n\ge 1$. Suppose that $a_n \notin P$, $a_k \in P$ for $0\le k < n$, and $a_0 \notin P^2$, then $f(x)$ is irreducible in $R[x]$. 

**Cor (Eisenstein's Criterion for $\Bbb Z[x]$):** Let $p$ be a prime in $\Bbb Z$ and let $f(x) = a_nx^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+ \dots + x_n\in\Bbb Z[x]$, with $n\ge 1$. Suppose that $p \not\mid a_n$, $p\mid a_k$ for $0\le k<n$, and $p^2 \not\mid a_0$, then $f(x)$ is irreducible in $\Bbb Z[x]$. If additionally, $f(x)$ is monic, $p\mid a_k$ for $0\le k<n$, and $p^2 \not\mid a_0$, then $f(x)$ is irreducible in both $\Bbb Z[x]$ and $\Bbb Q[x]$.
