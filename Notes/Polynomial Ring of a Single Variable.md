Let $R$ be a ring.

# Construction

Let us consider the abelian group $$R' := \bigoplus_{n \in \Bbb N} R.$$This is the set of functions from $\Bbb N$ to $R$ with finite support. We would like to give this group a multiplication that is inspired by the multiplication on $R$. To do this, let $(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N}, (b_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} \in R'$, $$(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} * (b_n)_{n \in \Bbb N} := \left(\sum_{k = 0}^n a_k b_{n-k}\right)_{n \in \Bbb N}.$$This operation is the *discrete convolution* of the two sequences. We can check that is associative and distributes over the sum. This means that $R'$ is a ring. Just a couple of things we should keep in mind. If $R$ is commutative, then it is easy to check that so is $R'$. Lastly, if $R$ is unital, then $R'$ is also unital with the multiplicative identity being $$(1, 0, 0 \dots).$$
We usually don't write $R'$. The usual notation is $R[x]$, and we write the element $(a_n)_{n \in \Bbb N}\in R'$ as $$\sum_{n = 0}^\infty a_n x^n,$$where $x$ is treated as a formal object. The elements of $R[x]$ are called *polynomials*. We call $R[x]$ the *ring of polynomials in the variable $x$ with coefficients in $R$*. 

# Properties

**Def:** Let $p(x) = \sum_{n = 0}^\infty a_n x^n$. We define the *degree of $p(x)$* to be $$\deg(p(x)) := \max\{n \in \Bbb N \mid a_n \neq  0\}. $$In this case, we call $a_n$ the *leading coefficient of $p(x)$* and $a_n x^n$ the *leading term of $p(x)$*, when $n = \deg(p(x))$. The polynomial is monic if their leading coefficient is $1$. We say that a polynomial is *constant* if their degree is $0$ 

The ring $R$ appears in $R[x]$ as the *constant polynomials*. Note that $R\not\subseteq R[x]$. This actually tells us that $R\hookrightarrow R[x]$ since there's an injective ring homomorphism. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative ring, and $p(x) = \sum_{k = 0}^\infty a_n x^n$
-  $p(x)$ is a unit iff $a_0$ is a unit in $R$ and $a_k$ is a zero divisor for $k \ge 1$. 
- $p(x)$ is nilpotent iff $a_k$ is nilpotent for $k \ge 0$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain and let $p(x), q(x) \in R[x]\setminus\{0\}$. Then:
- $\deg(p(x)q(x)) = \deg(p(x)) + \deg(p(x))$. 
- The units of $R[x]$ are just the units in $R$
- $R[x]$ is an integral domain. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a ring. We consider the evaluation function from $R[x]$ to $R$ to be $\text{eval}_a: R[x] \to R$ defined by $$p(x) = \sum_{n = 0}^\infty b_nx^n \mapsto b_n a^n,$$with $a\in R$. The evaluation function is a ring homomorphism iff $a\in Z(R)$. 

**Cor:** Let $R$ be a unital ring and $a, b\in R$. If $a\in Z(R)$, then $$(a+b)^n = \sum_{k = 0}^n {n \choose k} a^k b^{n-k},$$where ${n\choose k}$ is interpreted to be the $1+1+\dots + 1$ of the identity of $R$ taken ${n\choose k}$-times.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a commutative unital ring. The principal ideal generated by $x$ in the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is a prime ideal iff $R$ is an integral domain. Additionally, $(x)$ is maximal ideal iff $R[x]$ is a field. 

**Prop:** Assume $R$ is commutative. Let $f(x)$ be a monic polynomial in $R[x]$ of degree $n \ge 1$. 
- Every element of $R[x]/(f(x))$ is of the form $\overline{p(x)}$ for some polynomial $p(x)\in R[x]$ of degree less than $n$, i.e., $$R[x]/(f(x)) =  \left\{\left.\sum_{k = 0}^{n-1} \overline{a_kx^k} \;\right\rvert\;\forall 0\le i <n(a_i \in R)\right\}.$$
- If $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are distinct polynomials in $R[x]$ which are both of degree less than $n$, then $\overline{p(x)} \neq \overline{q(x)}$. 
- If $f(x) = a(x)b(x)$  where both $a(x)$ and $b(x)$ have degree less than $n$, then $\overline{a(x)}$ and $\overline{b(x)}$ are zero divisors in $R[x]/(f(x))$.
