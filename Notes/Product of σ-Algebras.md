**Def:** Let $\{X_\alpha \mid \alpha\in A\}$ be an index collection of sets, and $X:= \prod_{\alpha\in A} X_\alpha$, and $\pi_\alpha: X\to X_\alpha$ the coordinates maps. If ${\scr A}_\alpha$ is $\sigma$-algebra on $X_\alpha$ for each $\alpha$ the *product $\sigma$-algebra* on $X$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by  $$\{\pi^{-1}_\alpha[E_\alpha] \mid E\in {\scr A}_\alpha \land \alpha\in A\}. $$We denote this $\sigma$-algebra by $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in A} {\scr A}_\alpha$. If $A = \{1,\dots, n\}$ we also write $\bigotimes_1^n {\scr A}_j$ or ${\scr A}_1 \otimes \dots \otimes {\scr A}_n$.

**Prop:** If $A$ is countable, then $\bigotimes_{\alpha \in A} {\scr A}_\alpha$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $$\left\{\left.\prod_{\alpha \in A} E_\alpha\; \right\rvert\; E_\alpha \in {\scr A}_\alpha \right\}.$$
**Prop:** Suppose that ${\scr A}_\alpha$ is generated by $\cal E_\alpha$, $\alpha\in A$. Then $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in A}{\scr A}_\alpha$ is generated by ${\scr F}_1 := \{\pi^{-1}_\alpha[E_\alpha] \mid E_\alpha \in {\cal E}_\alpha\land \alpha\in A\}$. If $A$ is countable and $X_\alpha \in {\cal E}_\alpha$ for all $\alpha$, $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in A}{\scr A}_\alpha$ is generated by $${\scr F}_2 := \left\{\left.\prod_{\alpha \in A} E_\alpha\; \right\rvert\; E_\alpha \in {\cal E}_\alpha \right\}.$$
**Prop:** Let $X_1,\dots, X_n$ be topological spaces and let $X = \prod_1^n X_j$, equipped with the product topology. Then $$\bigotimes_{j = 1}^n \mathcal B(X_j) \subseteq \mathcal B(X).$$ If $X_j$'s are second countable, then $$\bigotimes_{j = 1}^n \mathcal B(X_j) = \mathcal B(X).$$
**Cor:** $\mathcal B(\Bbb R^n) = \bigotimes_1^n \mathcal B(\Bbb R)$.

We see that the product of $\sigma$-algebras is actually the coarsest $\sigma$-algebra such that when considering the product space the projections are measurable functions. Additionally, we note that it is similar to the [[Product Topology]].
