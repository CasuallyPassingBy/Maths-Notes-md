If $G = S_p$, with $p$ prime, then we know that $|S_p| = p (p-1)!$, and $p \not\mid (p-1)!$, thus the Sylow $p$-groups of $S_p$ must have order exactly $p$. Thus, the Sylow $p$-groups must be cyclic, and $S_p$ has exactly $(p-1)!$ number of $p$-cycles.  If $n_p$ is the number of Sylow $p$-groups, then $n_p (p-1) = (p-1)!$, and $n_p = (p-2)!$. Lastly, by Sylow's third theorem, we know that $n_p \equiv 1 \pmod p$, and $(p-2)! \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Getting another proof of [[Wilson's Theorem]].
