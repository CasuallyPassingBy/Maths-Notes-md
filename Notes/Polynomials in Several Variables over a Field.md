# Properties

For this note, $F$ is always a field.

**Def:** A unital commutative ring $R$ is called *Noetherian* if every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated.

**Hilbert's Basis Theorem:** If $R$ is Noetherian ring then so is the polynomial ring $R[x]$. 

**Cor:** Every ideal in the polynomial ring $F[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ with coefficients from a field is finitely generated. 

**Def:** A *monomial ordering* is a well ordering '$\ge$' on the set of monomials that satisfy $mm_1 \ge mm_2$ whenever $m_1 \ge m_2$ for monomials $m, m_1, m_2$. Equivalently, a monomial ordering may be specified by defining a well ordering on the $n$-tuples $\alpha = (a_1,\dots, a_n) \in \Bbb Z^n$ of multidegrees of monomials $Ax_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ that satisfies $\alpha+ \gamma \ge \beta+\gamma$ if $\alpha\ge \beta$ .

**Example:** 
- *Lexicographical monomial ordering:* We are considering $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$. We first declare an odering of the variables, for example, $x_1>x_2>\dots>x_n$ and then declaring that the monomial term $Ax_1^{a_1}\dots x_n^{a_n}$ with exponents $(a_1,\dots, a_n)$ has higher order then the monomial term $Bx_1^{b_1}\cdots x_n^{b_n}$ with exponents $(b_1,\dots, b_n)$ if the first component where the $n$-tuples differ has $a_k > b_k$. 

**Def:** Fix a monomial ordering on the polynomial ring $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$.
- The *leading term* of a nonzero polynomial $f(x) \in F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, denoted $\text{LT}(f)$. is the monomial therm of maximal order in $f$ and the leading term of $f = 0$ is $0$. Define the *multidegree of $f$*, denoted $\partial (f)$, to be the multidegree of the leading term of $f$.
- If $I$ is an ideal in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, the *ideal of leading terms*, denoted $\text{LT}(I)$, is the ideal generated by the leading terms of all the elements of the ideal $$\text{LT}(I) := (\text{LT}(f) \mid f\in I).$$
**Def:** Let $I$ be an ideal in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$. The ideal $\text{LT}(I)$ is by definition generated by monomials. Such ideals are called *monomial ideals* and are typically much easier to work with than generic ideals. 

**Prop:** Let $f, g\in F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$. We have the following properties.
- $\partial (fg) = \partial(f) + \partial(g)$
- $\text{LT}(fg) = \text{LT}(f) + \text{LT}(g)$. 

**Obs:** We can choose $f_1(x, y), f_2(x, y) \in F[x, y]$ with lexicographical ordering $x>y$ such that $I := (f_1, f_2)$, and $(\text{LT}(f_1), \text{LT}(f_2))\subset\text{LT}(I)$. We would like to get a set of generators of an ideal in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$ whose leading terms generate the leading terms of all the elements in $I$. 

**Def:** A *Gröbner basis* for an ideal $I$ in the polynomial ring $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$ is a finite set of generators $\{g_1,\dots, g_m\}$ for $I$ whose leading terms generate the ideal of all leading terms in $I$, i.e., $$I = (g_1,\dots, g_m) \quad \text{and}\quad \text{LT}(I) =(\text{LT}(g_1), \dots,\text{LT}(g_m)). $$

Note that a Gröbner basis is in fact a set of *generators* for $I$, meaning, that every element in $I$ is linear combination of the generators, not necessarily unique. 

**General Polynomial Division:** Fix a monomial ordering on $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, and suppose $g_1,\dots, g_m$ is a set of nonzero polynomials in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$. If $f$ is any polynomial in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, start with a set of quotients $q_1,\dots, q_n$ and a remainder $r$ initially all equal to $0$ and successively test whether the leading therm of the dividend $f$ is divisible by the leading terms of the divisors $g_1, \dots, g_m$ in that order. Then:
- If $\text{LT}(f)$ is divisible by $\text{LT}(g_i)$, say $\text{LT}(f) = a_i\text{LT}g_i$, and $a_i$ to the quotient $q_i$ replce by the dividend $f-a_i g_i$ (a polynomial of lower order leading term), and reiterate the entire process.
- If the leading term of the dividend $f$ is not divisible by any of the leading terms $\text{LT}(g_1), \dots,\text{LT}(g_m)$, add the leading term of $f$ to the remainder $r$, replace $f$ by the dividend $f-\text{LT}(f)$, and reiterate the entire process.
The process terminates when the dividend is $0$ and result in a set of quotients $q_1,\dots, q_m$ and a remainder $r$ with $$f = q_1g_1 +\dots q_m g_m +r.$$Each $q_i g_i$ has multidegree less than or equal to the multidegree of $f$ and the remainder $r$ has the property that no nonzero term in $r$ is divisible by any of the leading terms $\text{LT}(g_1), \dots,\text{LT}(g_m)$. 

**Th:** Fix a monomial ordering on $R = F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, and suppose $\{g_1,\dots, g_m\}$ is a Gröbner basis for the nonzero ideal $I$ in $R$: Then
- every polynomial $f\in R$ can be written uniquely in the form $$f = f_I+r,$$where $f_I\in I$ and no nonzero monomial term of the 'remainder' $r$ is divisble by any of the leading terms $\text{LT}(g_1), \dots,\text{LT}(g_m)$. 
- Both $f_I$ and $r$ can be computed by general polynomial division by $g_1,\dots, g_m$ and are independent of the order in which these polynomials are used in the division.
- The remainder $r$ proviced a unique representation for the coset of $f$ in the quotient ring $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]/I$. In particular, $f\in I$ iff $r= 0$. 

**Prop:** Fix a monomial ordering on $R = F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$ and let $I$ be a nonzero ideal in $R$.
- If $g_1,\dots, g_m$ are any elements of $I$ such that $\text{LT}(I) =(\text{LT}(g_1), \dots,\text{LT}(g_m))$, then $\{g_1,\dots, g_m\}$ is a Gröbner basis for $I$.
- The ideal $I$ has a Gröbner basis. 

A very natural question is how to test whether a fiven set of generators of an ideal $I$ is a Gröbner basis, which we then use to provide an algorithm to find a Gröbner basis. 

**Def:** If $f_1, f_2$ are two polynomials in $F[x_1,\dots, x_n]$ and $M$ is the monic least common multiple of the terms $\text{LT}(f_1)$ and $\text{LT}(f_2)$ then we can cancel the leading terms by taking the difference $$S(f_1,f_2) := \frac M{\text{LT}(f_1)} f_1- \frac M{\text{LT}(f_2)} f_2.$$

Then $$h = \sum_{k = 2}^m b_k S(f_{k-1}, f_k),$$for some constants $b_k \in F$.
