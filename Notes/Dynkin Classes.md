**Def:** Let $X$ be a set. A collection $\cal D$ of subsets of $X$ is a $d$-system or a *Dynkin class*, on $X$ if
- $X\in \cal D$,
- if $A, B\in \cal D$, and $A\subseteq B$, then $A\setminus B\in \cal D$, and
- If $\{A_n\}_{n<\omega}$ is an increasing sequence of sets in $\cal D$, then $\bigcup_{n<\omega}A_n \in \cal D$.

Suppose that $X$ is set and that $\cal A$ is a $\sigma$-algebra on $X$. If $\mu$ and $\nu$ are finite measures on $\cal A$ such that $\mu(X) = \nu(X)$, then the collection $\cal S$ of all sets $A$ that belong to $\cal A$ and satisfy $\mu(A) = \nu(A)$ is a $d$-system. 

**Obs:** The intersection of a nonempty family of $d$-systems on a set $X$ is a $d$-system on $X$ and that an arbitrary collection of subsets of $X$ is included in some $d$-system on $X$, namely the collection $\mathcal P(X)$. 

If $\cal C$ is an arbitrary collection of subsets of $X$, then the intersection of all $d$-systems on $X$ that include $\cal C$ is a $d$-system on $X$ that includes $\cal C$; this intersection is the smallest $d$-system and is called the $d$-system generated by $\cal C$. We will sometimes denote this $d$-system by $d({\cal C}).$

**Th:** Let $X$ be a set, and let $\cal C$ be a $\pi$-system on $X$. Then the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\cal C$ coincides with the $d$-system generated by $\cal C$.

**Cor:** Let $(X, {\cal A})$ be a measurable space, and let $\cal C$ be a $\pi$-system on $X$ such that $\cal A= \sigma({\cal C}).$ If $\mu$ and $\nu$ are finite measures on $\cal A$ that satisfy $\mu(X) = \nu(X)$ and that satisfy $\mu(C) = \nu(C)$ for each $C\in \cal C$, then $\mu = \nu$. 

**Cor:** Let $(X, {\cal A})$ be a measurable space, and let $\cal C$ be a $\pi$-system on $X$ such that ${\cal A} = \sigma({\cal C})$. If $\mu$ and $\nu$ are measures on $(X, {\cal A})$ that agree on ${\cal C}$, and if there is an increasing sequence $\{C_n\}_{n<\omega}$ of sets belong to $\cal C$, have finite measure under $\mu$ and $\nu$, and satisfy $\bigcup_{n<\omega} C_n = X,$ then $\mu = \nu$.
