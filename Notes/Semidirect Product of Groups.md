**Def:** Given two normal subgroup $K, Q\trianglelefteq G$, such that $KQ = G$ and $K \cap Q = \{e\}$, we say that $G$ is the direct product of $Q$ and $K$. We can relax the conditions such that $K \trianglelefteq G$ and $Q \le G$ such that $K \cap Q = \{e\}$ and $KQ = G$, we say that $G$ is the *semidirect product of $K$ by $Q$* and we will denote it by $$G = K \rtimes Q= Q \ltimes K.$$

If $K \trianglelefteq G$ and $Q'$ is a group isomorphic to $Q\le G$ such that $K \cap Q = \{e\}$ and $KQ = G$, we say that $G$ is the *semidirect product of $K$ by $Q'$*, and we also denote it as $G = K\rtimes Q'$. This will be the definition we will be using. 

**Obs:** If $G = K \rtimes Q$, we know that $K \cap Q \trianglelefteq Q$ and $Q/(K \cap Q)\cong KQ/K$, and because $K \cap Q =\{e\}$ and $KQ =G$, then $G \cong G/K$, meaning that the semidirect product $G = K \rtimes Q$ is necessarily of the form $$ G = K \rtimes G/K.$$

**Examples:** 
- We know that if $G = S_n$, and $K = A_n \trianglelefteq S_n$, then $S_n/A_n \cong \Bbb Z/2\Bbb Z$ has order $2$. Since, $\Bbb Z/2\Bbb Z$ is isomorphic to the group generated by a transposition, say $H = \langle (1,2) \rangle$ and we have $H \cap A_n = \{()\}$ and $S_n = A_n H$, then we have  $$S_n = A_n \rtimes C(2).$$
- If $G = D_n$, and $K = R_n$ (the rotation subgroup), then $R_n \trianglelefteq D_n$, and $[D_n: R_n] = 2$. Note that $\Bbb Z/2\Bbb Z$ is isomorphic to the subgroup $H$ generated by any reflection. We have that $$ D_n = R_n \rtimes C(2).$$
- If $G = S_3$. and $K = \langle (1,2,3) \rangle$, let us see that $[S_3: K] = 2$, and thus $K \trianglelefteq S_3$. If $H$ is the subgroup of $S_3$ generated by a transposition $(1,2)$, $H$ is cyclic of order $2$, and we have that $$S_3 = \langle (1,2,3) \rangle \rtimes \langle (1,2) \rangle = C(3)\rtimes C(2)$$Let us note that $C(6)$ that $C(6) = C(3) \times C(2),$ and thus $C(6) = C(3) \rtimes C(2)$. 

**Lemma:** Suppose $G = K \rtimes Q$. Then 
- We have an action $Q \times K \to K$ given by $\sigma*k = \sigma k \sigma^{-1}$, which is usually written as $\sigma*k = k^\sigma$. This action is linear in each of its variables:
	- if $k, k'\in K$, and $\sigma \in Q$, then $(kk')^\sigma = k^\sigma k'^\sigma$.
	- If $k \in K$ and $\sigma, \tau\in Q$, then $k^{(\sigma\tau)} = (k^\tau)^\sigma$. 
	Note that the exponentiation order matters. 
- If $g= k\sigma$ and $g' = k'\sigma'$ are elements of $G = KQ$, with $k, k'\in K$ and $\sigma, \sigma'\in Q$, then the product $gg'$ in $G$ is given by $$gg' = (k k'^\sigma) (\sigma \sigma'),$$where $k'^\sigma$ is the action of $\sigma$ on $k$. 

**Def:** We say that a bilinear action $Q\times K \to K$ *realises* the semidirect product $G = K \rtimes Q$. Supongamos now that $K, Q$ are two groups and we have a bilinear action of $Q$ on $K$, $\theta: Q \times K \to K$. We define $$K \rtimes_\theta Q := K \times Q$$with the binary operation $$(\sigma, x) \cdot(\tau, y) = (\sigma \tau^x, xy),$$where $\tau^x := \theta(x, \tau)$. Note that $x, y\in Q$, $\tau^{xy} = xy*\tau = x*(y*\tau) =(\tau^y)^x$, if $\sigma, \tau\in K$, then $(\sigma\tau)^x = x*(\sigma \tau) = (x\sigma)(x\tau) = \sigma^x\tau^x$. 

**Th:** Let $K$ and $Q$ be groups, and $\theta: Q \times K \to K$ is a bilinear action. The following statements are true:
- $G = K \rtimes_\theta Q$ is a group.
- $K \cong K' := K \times\{1\} G$ and $Q \cong Q' := \{1\}\times Q \le G$. 
- $G = K' \rtimes Q'$ is the semidirect product realises $\theta$. 

**Th:** If $G = K \rtimes Q$ is the semidirect product of $K$ by $Q$, then $G \cong K \rtimes_\theta Q$, for some action $\theta$ of $Q$ on $K$.
