Let $G$ be a Lie group. We see know that $\ell_g: G\to G$ is a diffeomorphism with inverse $\ell_{g^{-1}}$. The diffeomorphism $\ell_g$ maps $e\mapsto g$, and induces an isomorphism of tangent spaces: $$d(\ell_g)_e = \ell_{g*}: T_e G \to T_g G$$Thus, if we can describe the tangent space $T_e G$ at the identity, then $\ell_{g*}[T_e G]$ will give a description of the tangent space $T_g G$ at any point $g\in G$. 

**Def:** Let $X$ be a vector field on a Lie group $G$. We do not assume $X$ to be smooth. For any $g\in G$, the pushforward $\ell_{g*} X$ is a well-defined vector field on $G$. We say that the vector field $X$ is *left-invariant* if $$\ell_{g*} X = X$$for every $g\in G$; this means for any $h \in G$, $$\ell_{g*}(X_h) = X_{gh}.$$In other words, a vector field $X$ is left-invariant iff it is $\ell_g$-related to itself

**Prop:** Let $G$ be a Lie group, and suppose $X$ and $Y$ are smooth left-invariant vector fields on $G$. Then $[X, Y]$ is also left-invariant.

**Def:** The Lie algebra of all smooth left-invariant vector fields on a Lie group $G$ is called the *Lie algebra of $G$*, and is denoted as $\text{Lie}(G)$. 

**Prop:** $\text{Lie}(G)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak X(G)$. 

**Obs**: A left-invariant vector field $X$ is completely determined by its value $X_e$ at the identity, since $X_g = \ell_{g*} (X_e)$. Conversely, given a tangent vector $A \in T_e G$ we can define a vector field $\tilde A$ on $G$ by $(\tilde A)_g = \ell_{g*} A$. Then $\tilde A$ is left-invariant. This idea is stated more succinctly in the next theorem.  

**Th:** Let $G$ be a Lie group. The evaluation map $\varepsilon: \text{Lie}(G) \to T_e G$, given by  $\varepsilon(X) := X_e$, is a vector space isomorphism. Thus, $\text{Lie}(G)$ is a finite-dimensional equal to $\dim G$. 

**Cor:** Every left-invariant rough vector field on a Lie group is smooth.

**Cor:** Every Lie group admits a left-invariant smooth global frame, and therefore every Lie group is parallalisable.

**Lie Algebra of the General Linear Group:** The composition of natural maps $$\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(n,\Bbb R)) \to T_{I_n} \text{GL}(n, \Bbb R) \to {\frak gl}(n,\Bbb R) $$gives a Lie algebra isomorphism between $\text{Lie}(\text {GL}(n,\Bbb R))$ and the matrix algebra ${\frak gl}(n,\Bbb R)$. 

**Cor:** If $V$ is any finite dimensional real vector space, the composition of the canonical isomorphisms  $$\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(V)) \to T_{\text{id}} \text{GL}(V) \to {\frak gl}(V)$$yields a Lie algebra isomorphism between $\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(V))$ and ${\frak gl}(V)$. 

**Prop:** Suppose $G$ and $H$ are Lie groups. We see that $\text{Lie}(G \times H)$ is isomorphic to $\text{Lie}(G) \oplus \text{Lie}(H)$. 

**Prop:** If $G$ is an abelian Lie group, then $\text{Lie}(G)$ is abelian. 

# Induced Lie Algebra Homomorphism

**Def:** Given $A, B \in T_e G$, we define the Lie bracket $[A, B]\in T_e G$ as $$[A, B] := [\tilde A,\tilde B]_e $$where $\tilde A$ and $\tilde B$ are the left-invariant vector fields generated by $A$ and $B$, respectively.

**Prop:** If $A, B\in T_e G$ and $\tilde A, \tilde B$ are the left-invariant vector fields they generate, then  $$[\tilde A, \tilde B] = [A, B]\tilde\;. $$
With the Lie bracket $[\;, \;]$, the tangent space $T_e G$ becomes a Lie algebra, called the *Lie algebra of the Lie group $G$*. As the Lie algebra, $T_e G$ is usually denoted as $\mathfrak g$. 

**Induced Lie Algebra Homomorphisms:** Let $G$ and $H$ be Lie groups, and let $\frak g$ and $\frak h$ be their Lie algebras. Suppose $F: G \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism. For every $X\in \frak g$, there is a unique vector fields in $\frak h$ that is $F$-related to $X$. With this the vector field denoted by $F_* X$, the map $F_*: \frak g \to h$. so defined is a Lie algebra homomorphism. 

The map $F_*: \frak g \to h$ whose existence is asserted in the theorem above is called the *induced Lie algebra homomorphism.* 

**Properties of Induced Homomorphism:**
- The homomorphism $(\text{id}_G)_*: \text{Lie}(G) \to \text{Lie}(G)$ induced by the identity map of $G$ is the identity of $\text{Lie}(G)$.
- If $F_: G \to H$ and $F_2: H \to K$ are Lie group homomorphisms, then  $$(F_2 \circ F_1)_* = (F_2)_* \circ (F_1)_*: \text{Lie}(G) \to \text{Lie}(K). $$
- Isomorphic Lie groups have isomorphic Lie algebras.

The preceding proposition can be read in the light of category theory. Let $\sf Lie$ denote the category whose objects are Lie groups and whose morphisms are Lie group homomorphisms, and $\sf lie$ the one whose objects are finite dimensional Lie algebras and whose morphisms are Lie algebra homomorphism. Then the proposition above can be interpreted as showing that the assignments $G \mapsto \text{Lie}(G)$, $F\mapsto F_*$ define a covariant functor from $\sf Lie$ to $\sf lie$.

We can deduce that Lie group homomorphism that are immerions induce injective Lie algebra homomorphisms, and submersions induce surjective Lie algebra homomorphisms. 

**Cor:** Let $G$ and $H$ are Lie groups, and suppose $F: G \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism that is a also a local diffeomorphism. Then the induced homomorphism $F_*:\text{Lie}(G) \to \text{Lie}(H)$ is an isomorphisms of Lie algebras. 

## The Lie Algebra of a Lie Subgroup

**The Lie Algebra of a Lie Subgroup:** Suppose $H \le G$ is a Lie subgroup, and $\iota: H \hookrightarrow G$ is the inclusion map. There is a Lie subalgebra ${\frak h} \le \text{Lie}(G)$ that is canonically isomorphic to $\text{Lie}(H)$, characterised by either of the following descriptions $$\begin{align*} {\frak h} &= \iota_*(\text{Lie}(H)) \\ &= \{X\in \text{Lie}(G) \mid X_e \in T_e H\}.\end{align*} $$
**The Lie Algebra of $\text{GL}(n,\Bbb C)$:** The composition maps $$\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(n, \Bbb C)) \stackrel{\varepsilon}{\longrightarrow } T_{I_n} \text{GL}(n, \Bbb C) \stackrel{\varphi}{\longrightarrow } {\frak gl}(n, \Bbb C),$$where $\varepsilon$ is the evaluation map and $\varphi$ is the usual identification between the tangent space to an open subset of a vector space and the vector space itself, yields a Lie algebra isomorphism between $\text{Lie}(\text{GL}(n,\Bbb C))$ and the matrix algebra ${\frak gl}(n,\Bbb C)$. 

**Prop:** Suppose $F: G \to H$ is a Lie group homomorphism. Then the kernel of $F_*:\text{Lie}(G)\to \text{Lie}(H)$ is the Lie algebra of $\ker F$. 

**Def:** If $\frak g$ is a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, a *(finite-dimensional) representation of $\frak g$* is a Lie algebra homomorphism $\varphi: {\frak g} \to {\frak gl}(V)$ for some finite-dimensional vector space $V$, where ${\frak gl}(V)$ denotes the Lie algebra of linear maps from $V$ to itself. If $\varphi$ is injective, it is said to be *faithful*, in which case $\frak g$ is isomorphic to a Lie subalgebra $\varphi[{\frak g}] \le {\frak gl}(V) \cong {\frak gl}(n,\Bbb R)$. 

$(*)$ **Ado's Theorem:** Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra admits a faithful finite-dimensional representation.

**Cor:** Every finite-dimensional real Lie algebra is isomorphic to a Lie subalgebra of some matrix algebra $\mathfrak{gl}(n,\Bbb R)$ with the commutator bracket.

# Left Invariant $k$-forms

**Def:** For $g\in G$, let $\ell_g: G \to G$ be left multiplication by $g$. A $k$-form $\omega$ on $G$ is said to be *left-invariant* if $\ell_g^* \omega = \omega$ for all $g \in G$. This means that for all $g, x \in G$, $$\ell_g^*(\omega_gx) = \omega_x.$$Thus, a left-invariant $k$-form is uniquely determined by its value at the identity, since for any $g\in G$, $$\omega_g = \ell_{g^{-1}}^* (\omega_e).$$
**Prop:** Every left-invariant $k$-form $\omega$ on a Lie group $G$ is smooth.

**Def:** Let $\Omega^k(G)^G$ denote the vector space of left-invariant $k$-forms in $G$. The linear map $$\Omega^k(G)^G \to {\textstyle \bigwedge}^{\! k}(\mathfrak g^*), \qquad \omega \mapsto \omega_e$$has an inverse and is therefore an isomorphisms. Then $\dim \Omega^k(G)^G = {n \choose k}$.
