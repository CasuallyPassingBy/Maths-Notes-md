Let $G$ be a Lie group. We see know that $\ell_g: G\to G$ is a diffeomorphism with inverse $\ell_{g^{-1}}$. The diffeomorphism $\ell_g$ maps $e\mapsto g$, and induces an isomorphism of tangent spaces: $$d(\ell_g)_e = \ell_{g*}: T_e G \to T_g G$$Thus, if we can describe the tangent space $T_e G$ at the identity, then $\ell_{g*}[T_e G]$ will give a description of the tangent space $T_g G$ at any point $g\in G$. 

**Def:** Let $X$ be a vector field on a Lie group $G$. We do not assume $X$ to be smooth. For any $g\in G$, the pushforward $\ell_{g*} X$ is a well-defined vector field on $G$. We say that the vector field $X$ is *left-invariant* if $$\ell_{g*} X = X$$for every $g\in G$; this means for any $h \in G$, $$\ell_{g*}(X_h) = X_{gh}.$$In other words, a vector field $X$ is left-invariant iff it is $\ell_g$-related to itself

**Obs**: A left-invariant vector field $X$ is completely determined by its value $X_e$ at the identity, since $X_g = \ell_{g*} (X_e)$. Conversely, given a tangent vector $A \in T_e G$ we can define a vector field $\tilde A$ on $G$ by $(\tilde A)_g = \ell_{g*} A$. Then $\tilde A$ is left-invariant. 

We call $\tilde A$ the *left-invariant vector field on $G$ generated by $A \in T_e G$*.  Let $\text{Lie}(G)$ be the vector space of all-left invariant vector fields on $G$. Then there's a bijection $T_e G \leftrightarrow \text{Lie}(G)(G)$ by $X \in \text{Lie}(G)$ then $X_e \in T_e G$ and $A\in T_e G$ to $\tilde A \in \text{Lie}(G)$. This correspondence is a vector space isomorphism.  

**Prop:** Any left-invariante vector field $X$ on a lie group $G$ is smooth. 

**Prop:** If $X$ and $Y$ are left-invariant vector fields on $G$, then so is $[X, Y]$.

**Prop:** $\text{Lie}(G)$ is a Lie subalgebra of $\mathfrak X(G)$. 

**Def:** Given $A, B \in T_e G$, we define the Lie bracker $[A, B]\in T_e G$ as $$[A, B] := [\tilde A,\tilde B]_e $$where $\tilde A$ and $\tilde B$ are the left-invariant vector fields generated by $A$ and $B$, respectively.

**Prop:** If $A, B\in T_e G$ and $\tilde A, \tilde B$ are the left-invariant vector fields they generate, then  $$[\tilde A, \tilde B] = [A, B]\tilde\;. $$
With the Lie bracket $[\;, \;]$, the tangent space $T_e G$ becomes a Lie algebra, called the *Lie algebra of the Lie group $G$*. As the Lie algebra, $T_e G$ is usually denoted as $\mathfrak g$. 

**Note**: Let $F: H \to G$ be a Lie group homomorphism. A left-invariant vector field $X$ on $H$ is generated by its value $A = X_e \in T_e H$ at the identity, so $X = \tilde A$. Since $F$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism, then $dF_e: T_e H \to T_e G$ is a linear map. The diagram

show clearly the existence of an induced linear map $F_*: \text{Lie}(H) \to \text{Lie}(G)$ on left-invariante vector fields as well as a way to define it.

**Def:** Let $F: H \to G$ be a Lie group homomorphism. Define $F_*: \text{Lie}(H) \to \text{Lie}(G)$ by $$F_* (\tilde A) = (dF_e A)\tilde\;$$for all $A\in T_e H$. 

**Prop:** If $F: H \to G$ is a Lie group homomorphism and $X$ a left-invariant vector field on $H$, then the left-invariant vector field $F_* X$ on $G$ is $F$-related to the left-invariant vector field $X$. 

**Def:** If $F: H \to G$ is a Lie group homomorphism and $X$ a left-invariant vector field on $H$, we call $F_* X$ the *pushforward of $X$ under $F$*. 

**Prop:** If $F: H \to G$ is a Lie group homomorphism, then its differential at the identity, $F_* = dF_e: T_e H \to T_e G$ is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

# Left Invariant $k$-forms

**Def:** For $g\in G$, let $\ell_g: G \to G$ be left multiplication by $g$. A $k$-form $\omega$ on $G$ is said to be *left-invariant* if $\ell_g^* \omega = \omega$ for all $g \in G$. This means that for all $g, x \in G$, $$\ell_g^*(\omega_gx) = \omega_x.$$Thus, a left-invariant $k$-form is uniquely determined by its value at the identity, since for any $g\in G$, $$\omega_g = \ell_{g^{-1}}^* (\omega_e).$$
**Prop:** Every left-invariant $k$-form $\omega$ on a Lie group $G$ is smooth.

**Def:** Let $\Omega^k(G)^G$ denote the vector space of left-invariant $k$-forms in $G$. The linear map $$\Omega^k(G)^G \to {\textstyle \bigwedge}^{\! k}(\mathfrak g^*), \qquad \omega \mapsto \omega_e$$has an inverse and is therefore an isomorphisms. Then $\dim \Omega^k(G)^G = {n \choose k}$.
