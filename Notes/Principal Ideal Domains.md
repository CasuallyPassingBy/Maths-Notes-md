**Def:** A *Principal Ideal Domain* (P.I.D.) is an integral domain in which every ideal is principal. 

**Obs:** Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain. 

**Prop:** The quadratic integer ring $\Bbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ is not a principal ideal domain. An example of an ideal that is not principal is $(3, 2+\sqrt{-5})$, $(3, 1+\sqrt{-5})$ 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain, and let $a$ and $b$ be nonzero elements of $R$. Let $d$ be the generator for the principal ideal generated by $a$ and $b$. Then.
- $d$ is a greatest common divisors of $a$ and $b$
- $d$ can be written as an $R$-linear combination of $a$ and $b$, i.e., there are elements $x$ and $y$ in $R$ with $$d = ax+by.$$
- $d$ is unique up to multiplication by a unit of $R$. 

**Prop:** Every nonzero prime ideal in principal ideal domain is a maximal ideal. 

**Cor:** A quotient of a principal ideal domain by a prime ideal is a principal ideal domain. 

**Cor:** If $R$ is any commutative ring such that the polynomials ring $R[x]$ is a principal ideal domain, or an Euclidean domain, then $R$ is necessarily a field. 

**Def:** Define $N$ to be *Dedekind-Hasse norm* if $N$ is a positive norm and for every nonzero $a, b\in R$ either $a$ is an element of ideal $(b)$ or there is a nonzero element in the ideal $(a,b)$ of norm strictly smaller than the norm of $b$, i.e., either $b$ divides $a$ in $R$ or there exists $s,t\in R$ with $0<N(sa-tb) < N(b)$. 

**Prop:** If the integral domain $R$ has a Dedekind-Hasse norm, then $R$ is a principal ideal domain.

**Prop:** Let $R$ be a integral domain. If the following two conditions hold then $R$ is a principal ideal domain:
- any two nonzero elements $a$ and $b$ in $R$ have a greatest common divisor which can be written in the form $ra+sb$ for some $r, s\in R$, and
- if $a_1, a_2, a_3,\dots$ are nonzero elements of $R$ such that $a_{i+1}\mid a_i$ for all $i$, then there is a positive integer $N$ such that $a_n$ is a unit times $a_N$ for all $n \ge N$. 

**Prop:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. If  every *prime* ideal in $R$ is principal, then $R$ is a principal ideal domain. 

**Prop:** In a principal ideal domain, two ideals $(a)$ and $(b)$ are [[Chinese Remainder Theorem for Rings|comaximal]] iff the greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$ is $1$, in which case $a$ and $b$ are said to be *coprime* or *relatively prime*. 

**Obs:** In a principal ideal domain, any two nonzero elements have a least common multiple. 

**Cor:** A quotient of a principal ideal domain by a prime ideal is a principal ideal domain. 

**Prop:** If $R$ is principal ideal domain and $D$ is multiplicatively closed subset, then $D^{-1}R$ is also a principal ideal domain. 

**Prop:** In a principal ideal domain a nonzero element is prime iff it is prime. 

**Th:** Every principal ideal domain is a[[Unique Factorisation Domains| unique factorisation domain]]. 

**Cor:** Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain. There exists a multiplicative Dedekind-Hasse norm on $R$. 

**Th:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. $R$ is principal ideal domain iff it has a Dedekind-Hasse norm. 

**Th:** Let $R$ be an integral domain. $R$ is a principal ideal domain iff it is a [[Bézout Domains|Bézout domain]] and unique factorisation domain. 

**Prop:** The following [[Quadratic Integers|quadratic integer]] rings are Principal Ideal Domains|principal ideal domains but not Euclidean Domains:
- $\Bbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-19})/2]$
- $\Bbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-43})/2]$
- $\Bbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-67})/2]$
- $\Bbb Z[(1+\sqrt{-163})/2]$
